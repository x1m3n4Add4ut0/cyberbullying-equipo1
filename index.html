What is cyberbullying?

It is harassment or intimidation carried out through digital technologies: social media, messaging, online games or email. It builds on the classic definition of bullying by researcher Dan Olweus, adapted to the digital world.

Intent

The aggressor seeks to harm, humiliate or exclude. It is not an accident or a joke between equals that both enjoy.

Repetition

A single message or image can keep circulating, being shared and viewed again and again, even if it was posted only once.

Power imbalance

Power can come from anonymity, popularity, number of followers or technical skill.

Causes and consequences

There are reasons behind harassment, but what matters is the real harm it leaves.

⚡ Why does it happen?

  • Anonymity and a sense of impunity.
  • Seeking popularity or group approval.
  • Lack of empathy from not seeing the victim's reaction.
  • Having experienced violence at home or at school.
  • Little supervision and digital education.

💔 Consequences

  • Anxiety, stress and constant sadness.
  • Depression and low self-esteem.
  • Isolation and fear of going to school.
  • Lower academic performance.
  • Self-harm or suicidal thoughts in severe cases.

🚨 Warning signs

  • Gets nervous when receiving notifications.
  • Suddenly stops using the phone or hides it.
  • Changes in mood, sleep or appetite.
  • Avoids going to class or to gatherings.
  • Withdraws from friends and family.
Remember: the aggressor also needs help. People who bully often struggle to manage emotions and are more prone to risky behavior if no one steps in.
